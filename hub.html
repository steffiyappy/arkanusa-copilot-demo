01Framing

The audience already has good models. Several teams pay for them. So the demonstration does not argue about model quality, it establishes parity early and then spends its time on the four things that actually vary: how much of the work the assistant can reach without an integration project, whether the output lands in the file or in the chat, whose identity it acts as, and what the estate looks like when twenty three companies each make their own choice.

Parity, not superiority. Do not claim the model is better, and do not call models a commodity either. This audience pays for two of them precisely because they know models differ by task. The accurate position is that frontier quality is now table stakes and task dependent, that Copilot itself offers model choice, and that on the tasks shown today the quality is not the variable. What varies is what the model is attached to and what happens around it.
Be accurate about the competition. Be accurate about what the tools they already pay for can now do, because getting this wrong is the fastest way to lose the room. ChatGPT Enterprise has company knowledge, connectors, apps and agents. Claude has enterprise connectors, MCP, and works inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook. Both have enterprise controls. Anyone who tells this audience that a competitor cannot reach enterprise data or cannot act in an application is about six months out of date, and someone in the room will know it.
On running it live. Five segments run fully live. The rest are pre-run with one live follow-up prompt, because generation latency is the most common way a demonstration of this kind fails. Pre-running is not cheating and you should say openly that you prepared it. What you must not do is stand in silence watching a progress indicator for four minutes.
Before you show segment nine. Position Scout carefully. Confirm your own preview entitlement and the preview terms before you show it, and do not present it as something the customer can procure on Monday. If you cannot stand behind an availability answer, cut the segment and close on scheduled and event driven tasks in Cowork instead, which makes the same point about moving from asking to being told.
The fictional account

Everything in this hub uses Arkanusa Group, a fictional diversified holding company with 23 portfolio companies across 6 clusters. The figures are internally consistent and every claim in the demonstration is provable from the supplied dataset.

Financial Services Special Situations and Asset Recovery Industrial Estates Infrastructure and Engineering Media, Publishing and Creative Manufacturing and Technology

02Storyboard

Four acts. Each act ends on the pivot that a standalone assistant cannot follow.

Act 1 · segments 1, 2

The question arrives

A real question lands. Copilot turns scattered source material into finished artefacts, then shows how much of the work estate it can reach without anyone building an integration.

Pivot: Their current tools can reach enterprise data too, through connectors somebody configured. The question to leave hanging is what that reach cost to build and who maintains it.
Act 2 · segments 3, 4

The judgement

Depth of reasoning, twice. First Researcher pulls a weak executive briefing apart. Then it convenes a council on a live strategic decision.

Pivot: This is the parity moment, and it is the act where competitors are strongest. Do not oversell it. Establish that quality is not the variable and move on.
Act 3 · segments 5, 6, 7

The work

The work happens where the work already is: inside Excel, inside the meeting, and then handed to an agent, with the identity and audit trail shown rather than asserted.

Pivot: Segment seven is where the argument is actually won, because it is the only place governance is demonstrated instead of described.
Act 4 · segments 8, 9

The scale

From one answer to a whole workstream. Cowork produces a multi-artefact pack against live data and live web research. Scout carries it into the operating rhythm.

Pivot: The question stops being what can it write, and becomes what the estate looks like when every subsidiary answers this question separately.

Researcher: critique the daily briefing

Researcher agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot.
7minutes
Fully live. Short prompts, reliable latency.
Show reasoning depth applied to the customer's own writing, not to a public dataset.
Prompts
This is a draft daily briefing for the group chief executive. Critique it. Do not rewrite it yet. Tell me where it is weak, where a claim is asserted without evidence, where the ordering buries something important, and where the reader is left without a decision to make.
Which single sentence in this briefing is most likely to be wrong, and what evidence would settle it either way?
Now rewrite it. Lead with the item that threatens the full year result. Keep it to one page. Every claim either carries a source or is marked as unverified. End with the specific decisions you are asking the chief executive to make today.
Talk track
  • Say what the briefing is before you run it: eight sections, reads fine, and is quietly useless.
  • Let the critique land without narrating it. Then pick the two findings the room reacts to.
  • The second prompt is the sharpest. It goes after the causal claim about material costs.
What to watch for
  • It finds the buried lede in section six.
  • It challenges the material cost explanation as asserted rather than evidenced.
  • It spots the stale occupancy figure quoted at May among July numbers.
  • It notes that the briefing asks the chief executive for nothing.
Files
Group_CEO_Daily_Briefing_19Aug2026_DRAFT.docx
If it goes wrong. Presenter answer key is in the demo files. If the model misses a finding, prompt for it directly rather than pretending it found it.
The differentiator in this segment. Be honest here. Strong models all critique text well and this is where competitors are most competitive. The point worth making is only that it is reading the briefing that was actually going to be sent.

Researcher, multi-perspective reasoning on a live decision.
5minutes
Pre-run the council. Run the third prompt live.
Show structured disagreement on a decision that matters to a holding company, grounded in the customer's own numbers.
Prompts
Convene a council on this question. Give me four distinct positions: an investment banker optimising for proceeds, a state shareholder optimising for policy control, a risk officer optimising for downside, and an operator optimising for the business itself. Each must argue from the numbers in the pack, and each must name the single fact that would change their mind.

What to watch for

  • It separates real disagreement from vocabulary.
  • It surfaces the uncomfortable framing: selling the highest margin asset to fund the lowest margin one.

Agent Builder

Agent built in Microsoft 365 Copilot over governance documents.
9minutes
Fully live. The most important segment in the session.
Show a business user, not a developer, building something governed and reusable.
Prompts
Build the agent live. Name it Bid Governance Adviser. Knowledge: the contract governance standard, the delegation of authority and the approval thresholds workbook. Instruction: answer only from these documents, always cite the clause, and say plainly when a proposed bid breaches a control.
Test it: a fixed price bid of 180 billion rupiah to a regional government client, with 5 percent contingency. Who approves it, and does it breach anything?
Test it again: we want to waive the independent estimate review to meet a tender deadline. Can we, and who can authorise that?
PROOF, NOT ASSERTION. Now sign in as a second user who does not have access to the delegation of authority document, and ask the same approval question. Show the room that the answer changes.
Now open the admin surface. Show this agent in the inventory, who owns it, what it can reach, and the Purview audit record of the prompts you just ran.
Talk track
  • Build it in front of them. The build is the demonstration, not the agent.
  • The second test should return a breach: 5 percent contingency against an 8 percent minimum.
  • The third test is the important one. The correct answer is that the review cannot be waived at all.
  • The fourth and fifth prompts are why this segment is nine minutes rather than seven. Everything else in the session can be argued about. A permission boundary that visibly changes the answer, and an audit record you can point at, cannot.
  • Say plainly that this is a declarative agent on the user's identity. If asked about custom engine agents, say they need their own security and lifecycle design. Do not bluff this.
What to watch for
  • It cites the clause reference, not a paraphrase.
  • It refuses the waiver rather than finding a way to be helpful.
  • It applies the more restrictive of two overlapping thresholds.
  • The second identity gets a materially different answer, because retrieval is trimmed to what that user can open.
  • The agent appears in the inventory with a named owner, and the prompts appear in the audit record.
Files
SOP-CG-004_Contract_Governance_Standard.docx Group_Delegation_of_Authority_Rev7.docx Bid_Approval_Thresholds.xlsx
If it goes wrong. Have the agent pre-built in a second window and the second identity already signed in on a second device. If the admin surface is slow, use a screenshot captured that morning and say that is what it is.
The differentiator in this segment. The only segment where governance is demonstrated rather than described. Be precise: this is a declarative agent running on the user's identity. Custom engine agents need their own security and lifecycle design, and saying so is what makes the rest credible.

04The five questions that decide it

Do not present a feature comparison. Give them five questions to ask of any assistant, including the ones they already pay for, and let the answers do the work.

1.How much of the work can it reach without a project?

Why it decides the outcomeEvery assistant can reach enterprise data now. The question is what it costs to get there. Reach through configured connectors is real reach, but it is scoped to what somebody configured, and it has to be maintained.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersFirst party access to the Microsoft Graph, so mail, chat, meetings, files and the relationships between them are available by default rather than by integration project, with the user's own permissions applied at retrieval.
Where you must be honestLine of business data outside Microsoft 365 still needs connectors, and content the user cannot open is content Copilot cannot use. This is a difference of default reach and integration effort, not a difference between access and no access.
What to invite them to askAsk what it took to make their current tool see last Thursday's meeting, and who maintains that.
Proved inSegment 2 · Microsoft 365 Copilot and Work IQ

2.Does the work land in the file, or in the chat?

Why it decides the outcomeMost knowledge work is not producing text. It is changing a model, revising a deck, or turning a meeting into commitments.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersNative editing inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook, with versioning, co-authoring, sensitivity labels and permissions preserved because the file never leaves the estate.
Where you must be honestCompetitors also have Office add-ins and can write to files. The claim is fidelity and the absence of an export and reimport step, not exclusivity.
What to invite them to askAsk what happens to a sensitivity label on a document when it goes out to an assistant and comes back.
Proved inSegment 5 · Edit in the app: Excel

3.When an agent acts, whose identity is it using?

Why it decides the outcomeThe moment an assistant takes action rather than giving advice, identity stops being a detail and becomes the control.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersThe declarative agent demonstrated here runs on the user's identity and cannot reach anything that user cannot reach. It is created, inventoried and retired through the same admin controls the organisation already operates.
Where you must be honestThis is true of declarative agents. Custom engine agents and agents that use their own service connections need their own security and lifecycle design, and that work is not automatic. Say so rather than let them discover it.
What to invite them to askAsk, for any assistant they use, which agents exist today, who approved each one, and what happens to them when the owner leaves.
Proved inSegment 7 · Agent Builder

4.Can you evidence what it did, six weeks later?

Why it decides the outcomeFor a state linked group this is not an IT question. It is a question the audit board will ask, and the answer has to be evidenced rather than asserted.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersPrompts and responses are handled under Microsoft 365 contractual commitments, are not used to train foundation models, and are subject to the same Purview audit, retention, eDiscovery and legal hold tooling as the rest of the estate.
Where you must be honestCompetitor governance exists. Microsoft now offers Purview connectors that bring some of it into scope, with the Claude connector in preview. The honest claim is depth and native sensitivity label and DLP integration, and that competitor governance requires separate configuration and still has gaps. Note also that Cowork's Purview coverage is not yet complete, and that web queries and third party agents leave the tenant by design.
What to invite them to askAsk them to produce the audit record of a prompt from six weeks ago and place it under legal hold, in whichever tool they use.
Proved inSegment 7 · Agent Builder

5.What does this look like across twenty three companies?

Why it decides the outcomeOne team with a subscription is an experiment. Twenty three companies choosing separately is a governance problem and a procurement problem at the same time.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersA common control architecture: one identity model, one policy and compliance framework, and one admin surface per tenant, with the option to consolidate rather than federate twenty three separate decisions.
Where you must be honestDo not claim one licence covers everyone. Licensing is per user, Cowork is usage based, and agent and analytics consumption is metered. If the group runs several tenants, and on six email estates it very likely does, those remain separate boundaries until a tenant consolidation project says otherwise. The opportunity is consolidation, not a claim that consolidation has already happened.
What to invite them to askAsk what their estate looks like in eighteen months if each subsidiary picks its own assistant, and who reconciles it.

05The data story

Presenter reference. This is what the dataset proves and the order in which it proves it.

The headline. Infrastructure and Engineering EBITDA margin fell from 11.4 percent in FY2024 to 8.2 percent in FY2025 to 3.1 percent in FY2026 H1, while revenue grew 20 percent. The cluster reports the cause as construction material inflation. The data does not support that.
The challenge you will get, and must concede. The strongest challenge from the room: cost plus contracts pass inflation straight to the customer, so of course their margin held. That objection is correct and should be conceded immediately. It is why the argument does not rest on that comparison.
What actually survives that challenge. Inflation is real but insufficient, and the naive test for it is wrong. Comparing fixed price against cost plus proves nothing on its own, because cost plus passes input inflation to the client while fixed price absorbs it, so a common cost shock would produce exactly that divergence. Two tests survive that objection. First, size: composite input inflation over the whole period was 6.3 percent against a fixed price cost variance of 19.0 percent, so even assuming full absorption and zero recovery, roughly 12.7 percentage points, or about IDR 3,025 bn of forecast cost, is not inflation. Second, a within-type control: mean cost variance on fixed price work climbs with the period in which it was bid, from 11.5 percent for work bid in FY2023, through 16.2 and 17.3 percent, to 20.8 percent at the mandate peak. Every one of those groups absorbs input inflation identically and their delivery periods overlap. What changed is how they were bid.

The chain, in the order the data reveals it

1
Incentive

The FY2024 growth mandate rewarded order book intake, not delivered margin. Bid win rate rose from 22.4 percent to 41.3 percent.

2
Mix

The win rate was bought by moving to fixed price lump sum turnkey terms. LSTK share of won order value rose from 31 percent to 68 percent, which moved cost overrun risk from the client to the group.

3
Execution

Fixed price cost variance runs plus 19.0 percent against bid. Within that single contract type, variance climbs with the period in which the work was bid: 11.5 percent for FY2023, 16.2 and 17.3 percent through FY2024, and 20.8 percent at the FY2025 peak. Gross margin on the same series falls from plus 0.6 percent to minus 13.4 percent. That is a bidding effect, not a price effect.

4
Governance

The decisions behind that gap are on the record. The tender discount ceiling was raised from 4 to 8 percent in July 2024 with no compensating change to independent estimate review, and contingency minimums were waived, including on the single worst contract.

5
Concentration

Wisma Karya holds 72 percent of fixed price exposure by value and regional government clients hold 64 percent of it, so entity, contract type and counterparty risk all landed in the same place.

6
Cash

Regional government days sales outstanding is 192 days against 60 days for private clients, and public sector settlement has slowed every year since FY2023. Receivables over 180 days grew from IDR 96 bn at December 2024 to IDR 623 bn at June 2026.

The verdict. Inflation contributed. It cannot carry the explanation. A change in what the organisation rewarded moved risk onto the balance sheet, governance controls that would have priced that risk were relaxed at the same moment, and the cash consequence arrived about eighteen months later. Repricing steel does not reach any of that.
