01Framing

The audience already has good models. Several teams pay for them. So the demonstration does not argue about model quality, it establishes parity early and then spends its time on the four things that actually vary: how much of the work the assistant can reach without an integration project, whether the output lands in the file or in the chat, whose identity it acts as, and what the estate looks like when twenty three companies each make their own choice.

Parity, not superiority. Do not claim the model is better, and do not call models a commodity either. This audience pays for two of them precisely because they know models differ by task. The accurate position is that frontier quality is now table stakes and task dependent, that Copilot itself offers model choice, and that on the tasks shown today the quality is not the variable. What varies is what the model is attached to and what happens around it.
Be accurate about the competition. Be accurate about what the tools they already pay for can now do, because getting this wrong is the fastest way to lose the room. ChatGPT Enterprise has company knowledge, connectors, apps and agents. Claude has enterprise connectors, MCP, and works inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook. Both have enterprise controls. Anyone who tells this audience that a competitor cannot reach enterprise data or cannot act in an application is about six months out of date, and someone in the room will know it.
On running it live. Five segments run fully live. The rest are pre-run with one live follow-up prompt, because generation latency is the most common way a demonstration of this kind fails. Pre-running is not cheating and you should say openly that you prepared it. What you must not do is stand in silence watching a progress indicator for four minutes.
Before you show segment nine. Position Scout carefully. Confirm your own preview entitlement and the preview terms before you show it, and do not present it as something the customer can procure on Monday. If you cannot stand behind an availability answer, cut the segment and close on scheduled and event driven tasks in Cowork instead, which makes the same point about moving from asking to being told.
The fictional account

Everything in this hub uses Arkanusa Group, a fictional diversified holding company with 23 portfolio companies across 6 clusters. The figures are internally consistent and every claim in the demonstration is provable from the supplied dataset.

02Storyboard

Four acts. Each act ends on the pivot that a standalone assistant cannot follow.

Act 1 · segments 1, 2

The question arrives

A real question lands. Copilot turns scattered source material into finished artefacts, then shows how much of the work estate it can reach without anyone building an integration.

Pivot: Their current tools can reach enterprise data too, through connectors somebody configured. The question to leave hanging is what that reach cost to build and who maintains it.
Act 2 · segments 3, 4

The judgement

Depth of reasoning, twice. First Researcher pulls a weak executive briefing apart. Then it convenes a council on a live strategic decision.

Pivot: This is the parity moment, and it is the act where competitors are strongest. Do not oversell it. Establish that quality is not the variable and move on.
Act 3 · segments 5, 6, 7

The work

The work happens where the work already is: inside Excel, inside the meeting, and then handed to an agent, with the identity and audit trail shown rather than asserted.

Pivot: Segment seven is where the argument is actually won, because it is the only place governance is demonstrated instead of described.
Act 4 · segments 8, 9

The scale

From one answer to a whole workstream. Cowork produces a multi-artefact pack against live data and live web research. Scout carries it into the operating rhythm.

Pivot: The question stops being what can it write, and becomes what the estate looks like when every subsidiary answers this question separately.

What is going to go wrong that nobody has told me about yet?

Everything anyone knows about this problem is in that folder. Before I write a word, tell me what the notes actually prove, what they only assert, and what is missing.
Why this prompt. Run this in plain Copilot Chat before you call any agent. It establishes the evidence base that all three agents will then work from. Type it, then say nothing until it finishes.
What good looks like. It separates evidence from opinion before producing anything. That distinction is the whole discipline of a board paper, and it is the first thing a rushed analyst drops.
Task 1.1Copilot Chat, Word agent
Working notes to a four page board paper
Use the Word agent.

You are drafting for the Group Chief Executive of Arkanusa Group ahead of the September board. Ground everything on IE_Cluster_Working_Notes_UNSTRUCTURED.docx and Arkanusa_FY2026H1_Portfolio_Review_Pack.xlsx. Do not use any figure that is not in those two files.

Produce a board paper in Word, maximum four pages, saved as IE_Board_Paper_Draft.docx. Structure it exactly as follows.
1. Executive summary. One paragraph, maximum 120 words, stating what happened, why, and what you are asking the board to approve. No preamble.
2. Recommendation. Three numbered recommendations, each with a named owner role, a date, and a measurable test of completion.
3. What the numbers show. Maximum one page. Lead with the margin series, then the contract mix, then the cash position.
4. What caused it. State the causal chain in order. Where a step is evidenced, cite the file, sheet or paragraph in brackets. Where a step is inferred, write [inferred] and say what evidence would confirm it.
5. What we are doing about it, and by when.
6. What could still go wrong. Three risks, each with the early indicator that would tell us it is materialising.

Rules. Every figure carries its source in brackets. Anything the notes only assert is labelled unverified in the text, not in a footnote. Name the control that failed, never the individual. Do not use the words significant, substantial, materially or broadly in line: give the number instead.

Language: our content is a mix of Bahasa Indonesia and English. Produce the output entirely in English. Where the source was in Bahasa Indonesia, translate it into natural English first and show only the English. Keep proper nouns untranslated: people, subsidiaries, sites, client names and contract references.

Do not ask me any clarifying questions. Proceed in one pass and produce the whole thing.
What good looks like. A real .docx in the tenant, not text in a chat window. It leads with the decision, carries a source on every number, and visibly marks the two or three places where the evidence runs out. Scroll to one bracketed citation and trace it back to the note it came from.
Reality check. If it pads to fill four pages, follow up with: "Cut it to three pages. Remove anything that does not inform one of the three recommendations."
Task 1.2Copilot Chat, Excel agent
Evidence appendix, one sheet per claim
Use the Excel agent.

Build the evidence appendix that sits behind that board paper, as a new workbook saved as IE_Evidence_Appendix.xlsx. Ground it on Arkanusa_FY2026H1_Portfolio_Review_Pack.xlsx and Arkanusa_Receivables_and_Settlement_Pack.xlsx.

Sheet 0, named Index. One row per following sheet, with three columns: sheet name, the single claim that sheet proves stated in one sentence, and the source file it draws from.
Sheet 1, Margin by cluster. FY2024, FY2025 and H1 FY2026 EBITDA margin for all six clusters, with a column chart, sorted worst movement first.
Sheet 2, Margin by company. The six Infrastructure and Engineering companies only, same three periods, with the movement in percentage points. Conditional formatting: red below zero, amber below eight percent.
Sheet 3, Contract mix. Share of won order value by contract type for each award period, as a stacked column chart.
Sheet 4, Cost variance. Mean cost variance and gross margin by contract type, and separately by award period within fixed price work only.
Sheet 5, Concentration. Fixed price exposure by subsidiary and by client type, in value and percentage.
Sheet 6, Receivables. Ageing over 180 days by month, and settlement days by client type.

Every sheet: freeze the header row, add an autofilter, format IDR billions with a thousands separator and no decimals, percentages to one decimal. Put the source file name in cell A1 of each sheet in grey italic.

Do not ask me any clarifying questions. Proceed in one pass and produce the whole thing.
What good looks like. Seven working sheets with live charts and formatting, each answering one question, with an index that tells an audit committee where to look. The point is not the charts, it is that the paper and the appendix agree with each other.
Reality check. If a chart is missing or wrong, follow up naming the sheet: "On Sheet 3, add a stacked column chart of contract type share by award period, with data labels."

Task 1.4Copilot Chat
Make the three agents check each other
Now compare the three artefacts you just produced: IE_Board_Paper_Draft.docx, IE_Evidence_Appendix.xlsx and the finished deck.

List every figure that appears in more than one of them and confirm it is identical in each. Where a number differs, show the three values side by side, say which one is right and why, and correct the other two.

What good looks like. This is the part experienced people notice. Hand-assembled board packs fail exactly here, on the third revision, when the deck and the paper stop agreeing. Ask the room how long that reconciliation normally takes them.
Talk track
  • Open the working notes and scroll them so the room sees the mess. Say plainly that this is what real source material looks like.
  • Run the cold open in plain Copilot Chat first. It establishes the evidence base that all three agents then work from.
  • Call the Word agent by name. Say why you are switching: you want a document, not a chat reply, and the specialist agent knows what a document is.

  • Finish on Task 1.4. Three agents, three formats, and they reconcile against each other. Ask the room how long that reconciliation normally takes them before a board meeting.
What to watch for
  • The Word agent reorders the notes into the sequence a board needs rather than the sequence they were written in.
  • The Excel agent produces a workbook you could hand to an audit committee, not a table pasted into chat.
  • The PowerPoint agent replaces the placeholder slides with real analysis and rewrites the closing slide to ask for a decision.
  • All three artefacts quote the same figures. Hand-assembled board packs routinely fail exactly there.

Task 3.1Researcher in Microsoft 365 Copilot, model picker on Auto
Structured critique of the draft daily briefing
Attached is Group_CEO_Daily_Briefing_19Aug2026_DRAFT.docx, a draft daily briefing about to go to the Group Chief Executive of Arkanusa Group.

Critique it. Do not rewrite it yet.

Return your critique as a table with these columns: section, the issue, why it matters to this reader, and severity rated high, medium or low. Cover at minimum:
- Ordering. Is the most consequential item where a busy reader will actually see it?
- Causation. Where does the briefing assert a cause without evidence, and what would it need to show to earn that claim?
- Sourcing. Where does a finding rest on a single source, particularly where that source is the subject of the finding?
- Internal consistency. Are there two numbers in this document that cannot both be true as presented?
- Currency. Is any figure quoted as at a different date to the rest?
- Vague quantifiers. List every instance of significant, substantial, broadly in line or similar, and state what number should replace it.
- Omission. What consequence of the main finding is not mentioned at all?
- Actionability. What is the reader being asked to decide? If nothing, say so.

After the table, give me the three findings you would fix first if you only had ten minutes, and say why those three.

Do not ask me any clarifying questions. Proceed in one pass and produce the whole thing.
What good looks like. It should find the buried lede in section six, the unevidenced material cost claim, the occupancy figure quoted at May among July numbers, and the fact that the briefing asks the chief executive for nothing. The presenter answer key lists all ten planted weaknesses.
Reality check. If it misses a planted finding, prompt for it directly rather than pretending it found it: "Look again at the occupancy figure in section four. Is it as at the same date as everything else?"

Researcher council: IPO or strategic partner

Researcher, multi-perspective reasoning on a live decision.
5minutes
Pre-run the council. Run the third prompt live.
Show structured disagreement on a decision that matters to a holding company, grounded in the customer's own numbers.
Business fit. Why this topic. An operational holding company for state assets faces exactly this question: the industrial estates cluster is the highest quality cash flow in the portfolio and the only asset a public market or a trade buyer would price at a premium, while the engineering cluster is consuming working capital. Whether to list it, sell a stake in it, or hold it is a live board question for any group with that shape, and it carries the political dimension that a purely commercial case study would miss. It also sets up the uncomfortable framing the council should surface: selling the best asset to fund the worst one.
Prompts
Cold openResearcher in Microsoft 365 Copilot, Model CouncilOpen with this
Demand a verdict, not a balanced view
Four people around this table disagree about this. Give me the strongest version of each argument, then tell me which one is right.
Why this prompt. Switch the model picker to Model Council before you run it, and say why: high stakes, and you want to see where models diverge.
What good looks like. Most council style answers stop at balance, which is safe and useless. Forcing a verdict is harder and is what an executive committee is paying for.
Task 4.1Researcher, Model Council
Four positions on the IPO versus partner decision
Ground on Industrial_Estates_Strategic_Options_Brief.docx and Industrial_Estates_Valuation_Pack.xlsx.

The question: should Arkanusa Group take the Industrial Estates cluster to IPO in FY2027, or divest a 30 percent strategic stake to an international estate operator?

Convene a council of four. Give each one a section.
1. An investment banker optimising for proceeds and certainty of completion.
2. A state shareholder optimising for policy control and public accountability.
3. A chief risk officer optimising for downside and execution risk.
4. An operator who has to run the estates the day after the transaction closes.

Each section must contain: the position in one sentence, three arguments each citing a specific figure from the pack, the strongest objection to their own position stated fairly, and the single fact that would make them change their mind.

Then a table comparing all four across: preferred route, cash to the holding company, time to complete, what they are willing to give up, and what they refuse to give up.

Finally, a section headed "Where this actually lands": your own verdict, the two conditions that would have to hold for it to be right, and what you would need to see in the next 60 days to confirm it.

Cite the pack for every figure. Where you use outside market knowledge, mark it clearly and say how confident you are.

Do not ask me any clarifying questions. Proceed in one pass and produce the whole thing.
What good looks like. Four positions that argue from figures in the pack rather than from generalities, and a verdict that commits. Watch for the operator section, which is the one most people leave out and the one that decides whether the deal works.
Reality check. Model Council uses more compute and typically runs two to three times longer than Critique. Start it, then talk over it. Do not watch the spinner.
Task 4.2Researcher
Separate real disagreement from vocabulary
Now compare the four positions.

Give me three lists.
1. Genuine disagreements. Points where they would give different answers to the same question, with what each would say and what evidence would resolve it.
2. False disagreements. Points where they are using different words for the same underlying position.
3. Shared blind spots. Anything all four have assumed without examining, which would change the answer if it were wrong.

The third list is the one I care about most. Be hard on it.
What good looks like. The disagreement map is the artefact worth keeping. It collapses a two hour debate into the two real choices, and the shared blind spots list is usually the most valuable output of the whole segment.
Task 4.3Researcher
Attack the house recommendation
The preparer of that brief recommends deferring the transaction decision until the Infrastructure and Engineering remediation plan is funded.

First, steel-man that recommendation. Give me the strongest possible case for it in one paragraph.

Then argue against it as hard as you can. Specifically address: the cost of delay expressed in foregone development, market timing risk over an eighteen month window, what a competitor does with that time, and whether linking the two decisions is sound reasoning or just cautious.

End with a one line answer: keep the recommendation, or change it, and why.
What good looks like. Tests whether it will attack the view in the document it was given. If it only ever agrees with its source, it is not usable for a board.

06The data story

Presenter reference. This is what the dataset proves and the order in which it proves it.

The headline. Infrastructure and Engineering EBITDA margin fell from 11.4 percent in FY2024 to 8.2 percent in FY2025 to 3.1 percent in FY2026 H1, while revenue grew 20 percent. The cluster reports the cause as construction material inflation. The data does not support that.
The challenge you will get, and must concede. The strongest challenge from the room: cost plus contracts pass inflation straight to the customer, so of course their margin held. That objection is correct and should be conceded immediately. It is why the argument does not rest on that comparison.
What actually survives that challenge. Inflation is real but insufficient, and the naive test for it is wrong. Comparing fixed price against cost plus proves nothing on its own, because cost plus passes input inflation to the client while fixed price absorbs it, so a common cost shock would produce exactly that divergence. Two tests survive that objection. First, size: composite input inflation over the whole period was 6.3 percent against a fixed price cost variance of 19.0 percent, so even assuming full absorption and zero recovery, roughly 12.7 percentage points, or about IDR 3,025 bn of forecast cost, is not inflation. Second, a within-type control: mean cost variance on fixed price work climbs with the period in which it was bid, from 11.5 percent for work bid in FY2023, through 16.2 and 17.3 percent, to 20.8 percent at the mandate peak. Every one of those groups absorbs input inflation identically and their delivery periods overlap. What changed is how they were bid.

The chain, in the order the data reveals it

1
Incentive

The FY2024 growth mandate rewarded order book intake, not delivered margin. Bid win rate rose from 22.4 percent to 41.3 percent.

2
Mix

The win rate was bought by moving to fixed price lump sum turnkey terms. LSTK share of won order value rose from 31 percent to 68 percent, which moved cost overrun risk from the client to the group.

3
Execution

Fixed price cost variance runs plus 19.0 percent against bid. Within that single contract type, variance climbs with the period in which the work was bid: 11.5 percent for FY2023, 16.2 and 17.3 percent through FY2024, and 20.8 percent at the FY2025 peak. Gross margin on the same series falls from plus 0.6 percent to minus 13.4 percent. That is a bidding effect, not a price effect.

4
Governance

The decisions behind that gap are on the record. The tender discount ceiling was raised from 4 to 8 percent in July 2024 with no compensating change to independent estimate review, and contingency minimums were waived, including on the single worst contract.

5
Concentration

Wisma Karya holds 72 percent of fixed price exposure by value and regional government clients hold 64 percent of it, so entity, contract type and counterparty risk all landed in the same place.

6
Cash

Regional government days sales outstanding is 192 days against 60 days for private clients, and public sector settlement has slowed every year since FY2023. Receivables over 180 days grew from IDR 96 bn at December 2024 to IDR 623 bn at June 2026.

The verdict. Inflation contributed. It cannot carry the explanation. A change in what the organisation rewarded moved risk onto the balance sheet, governance controls that would have priced that risk were relaxed at the same moment, and the cash consequence arrived about eighteen months later. Repricing steel does not reach any of that.
