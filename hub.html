01Framing

The audience already has good models. Several teams pay for them. So the demonstration does not argue about model quality, it assumes parity and then shows the four things a standalone assistant structurally cannot do: see the work, act inside the work, be governed like the work, and scale as part of the work.

Parity, not superiority. Do not argue that the model is better. Argue that the model is the commodity. Every serious assistant now reasons well, and the audience already knows that because they are paying for two of them. The difference is what the model is attached to.
Give credit first. Be straight with them about where the tools they already pay for are genuinely strong: raw model access, fast experimentation, developer ergonomics, and a low barrier for an individual who wants an answer right now. Conceding that openly is what buys you the right to make the rest of the argument.
The fictional account

Everything in this hub uses Arkanusa Group, a fictional diversified holding company with 23 portfolio companies across 6 clusters. The figures are internally consistent and every claim in the demonstration is provable from the supplied dataset.

Financial Services Special Situations and Asset Recovery Industrial Estates Infrastructure and Engineering Media, Publishing and Creative Manufacturing and Technology

02Storyboard

Four acts. Each act ends on the pivot that a standalone assistant cannot follow.

Act 1 · segments 1, 2

The question arrives

A real question lands. Copilot turns scattered source material into finished artefacts, then shows it can reason across the whole work estate rather than a pasted excerpt.

Pivot: This is the point at which a standalone assistant needs you to paste the context in. Copilot already has it, trimmed to what the user is allowed to see.
Act 2 · segments 3, 4

The judgement

Depth of reasoning, twice. First Researcher pulls a weak executive briefing apart. Then it convenes a council on a live strategic decision.

Pivot: This is the parity moment. The reasoning is as good as anything the audience is paying for elsewhere, and it is reading their documents rather than a paste.
Act 3 · segments 5, 6, 7

The work

The work happens where the work already is: inside Excel, inside the meeting, and then handed to an agent that anyone in the business can build.

Pivot: Nothing here is a chat window. This is the part that does not port to a browser tab.
Act 4 · segments 8, 9

The scale

From one answer to a whole workstream. Cowork produces a multi-artefact pack against live data and live web research. Scout carries it into the operating rhythm.

Pivot: The question stops being what can it write, and becomes how much of the job can it carry.

Copilot Chat with agent mode

Microsoft 365 Copilot app, agent mode. Word, Excel and PowerPoint output.
8minutes
Turn a pile of unstructured working notes into three finished artefacts without leaving the assistant.
Prompts
Read the working notes on the Infrastructure and Engineering cluster. They are unordered. Turn them into a four page board paper with a clear recommendation, a one paragraph executive summary at the top, and every figure attributed to its source. Do not add anything the notes do not support.
Using the Portfolio Review Pack, build me an evidence appendix in Excel. One sheet for contract mix, one for cost variance by contract type, one for concentration, one for receivables ageing. Add a summary sheet at the front that states in one line what each sheet proves.
The board deck stops at the symptom and has three placeholder slides. Finish it. Replace the placeholders with the analysis from the working notes, and rewrite the final slide so it asks the board for a specific decision.
Talk track
  • Open with the notes. Scroll them so the room sees they are a mess. Say plainly that this is what real source material looks like.
  • Run the Word prompt first. While it works, say what you asked for that most people forget to ask for: attribution, and a prohibition on inventing.
  • Do not read the output aloud. Scroll to one specific number and trace it back to the note it came from.
What to watch for
  • The paper reorders the notes into the sequence a board needs rather than the sequence they were written in.
  • Figures carry their source. Nothing is invented.
  • The deck's placeholder slides are replaced with real analysis, not filler.
Files
IE_Cluster_Working_Notes_UNSTRUCTURED.docx Arkanusa_FY2026H1_Portfolio_Review_Pack.xlsx IE_Board_Pack_Section4_DRAFT_INCOMPLETE.pptx
If it goes wrong. If agent mode is slow, run the Word prompt only and show the pre-built evidence appendix from the demo files instead.
The differentiator in this segment. A standalone assistant can do this too, if you paste everything in. Note how much you did not paste.

Microsoft 365 Copilot and Work IQ

Microsoft 365 Copilot, grounded across mail, chat, meetings and files.
7minutes
Show reasoning across the whole work estate, with permission trimming intact.
Prompts
Pull together everything across my documents about the Infrastructure and Engineering margin position. Give me a timeline of who knew what and when, and cite the source for each entry.
Was this risk raised before it became a problem? If it was, tell me who raised it, on what date, what they specifically asked for, and what happened to each of their requests.
Now tell me what is missing. What would you need to see, that you cannot currently find, to be confident about the cause?
Talk track
  • Frame it before you run it: the question is not can it write, it is what can it see.
  • The timeline answer is the moment. It stitches a committee minute, a risk memo and a management account into one narrative.
  • The third prompt is the credibility play. A tool that tells you what it does not know is a tool you can put in front of a board.
What to watch for
  • It finds the February 2025 risk memo and connects it to the May 2026 committee item.
  • It notes that four specific requests were made and none implemented.
  • Every claim carries a source you can click.
Files
Group_Bid_Committee_Minutes_Extracts.docx CRO_Memo_Fixed_Price_Concentration.docx
If it goes wrong. If tenant grounding is unavailable, upload the two documents and run the same three prompts. Say openly that you are simulating the estate.
The differentiator in this segment. This is the structural difference. The answer quality depends on reach, and reach depends on being inside the tenant with the user's permissions applied.

Researcher: critique the daily briefing

Researcher agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot.
7minutes
Show reasoning depth applied to the customer's own writing, not to a public dataset.
Prompts
This is a draft daily briefing for the group chief executive. Critique it. Do not rewrite it yet. Tell me where it is weak, where a claim is asserted without evidence, where the ordering buries something important, and where the reader is left without a decision to make.
Which single sentence in this briefing is most likely to be wrong, and what evidence would settle it either way?
Now rewrite it. Lead with the item that threatens the full year result. Keep it to one page. Every claim either carries a source or is marked as unverified. End with the specific decisions you are asking the chief executive to make today.
Talk track
  • Say what the briefing is before you run it: eight sections, reads fine, and is quietly useless.
  • Let the critique land without narrating it. Then pick the two findings the room reacts to.
  • The second prompt is the sharpest. It goes after the causal claim about material costs.
What to watch for
  • It finds the buried lede in section six.
  • It challenges the material cost explanation as asserted rather than evidenced.
  • It spots the stale occupancy figure quoted at May among July numbers.
  • It notes that the briefing asks the chief executive for nothing.
Files
Group_CEO_Daily_Briefing_19Aug2026_DRAFT.docx
If it goes wrong. Presenter answer key is in the demo files. If the model misses a finding, prompt for it directly rather than pretending it found it.
The differentiator in this segment. Any strong model can critique text. Only this one is reading the briefing that was actually going to be sent, in the place it was going to be sent from.

Researcher council: IPO or strategic partner

Researcher, multi-perspective reasoning on a live decision.
8minutes
Show structured disagreement on a decision that matters to a holding company, grounded in the customer's own numbers.
Prompts
Convene a council on this question. Give me four distinct positions: an investment banker optimising for proceeds, a state shareholder optimising for policy control, a risk officer optimising for downside, and an operator optimising for the business itself. Each must argue from the numbers in the pack, and each must name the single fact that would change their mind.
Where do the four genuinely disagree, and where are they only using different words for the same position?
The preparer recommends deferring the transaction decision until the Infrastructure remediation plan is funded. Steel-man that recommendation, then give me the strongest case against it.
Talk track
  • Introduce the decision in one sentence. Do not read the brief.
  • When the council returns, go straight to the disagreement map. That is the artefact an executive committee actually wants.
  • Close on the third prompt. The point is that it will argue against the house view when the evidence supports it.
What to watch for
  • Each position argues from a figure in the pack, not from generalities.
  • It separates real disagreement from vocabulary.
  • It surfaces the uncomfortable framing: selling the highest margin asset to fund the lowest margin one.
Files
Industrial_Estates_Strategic_Options_Brief.docx Industrial_Estates_Valuation_Pack.xlsx
If it goes wrong. If the council format is unavailable, run the same prompt as a single request for four labelled positions.
The differentiator in this segment. Reasoning parity is the assumption here, not the claim. The claim is that it is reasoning over the actual valuation pack.

Edit in the app: Excel

Copilot in Excel, on a live driver-based model.
6minutes
Show work happening inside the file, with formulas intact.
Prompts
Explain what this model does and which inputs actually move the answer. Rank the drivers by how much a ten percent change in each moves FY2028 EBITDA.
Build me a downside case. Fixed price share falls only to 50 percent instead of 25, cost variance improves only to 15 percent, and regional government payment terms do not improve at all. Show the result next to the current case.
Add a short written commentary under the projection explaining, in plain language a non-financial board member would follow, why the cash released figure is larger than the profit improvement.
Talk track
  • Show the Drivers sheet first. Amber cells are inputs, everything else is a formula.
  • Run the sensitivity prompt. The answer that matters is that contract mix beats every other lever.
  • The third prompt is the one finance teams feel. It writes the commentary that nobody has time to write.
What to watch for
  • It reads the formula chain rather than just the values.
  • The downside case is written into the sheet, not described in chat.
  • Cash released exceeds the EBITDA improvement, and it explains why.
Files
Arkanusa_IE_Turnaround_Scenario_Model.xlsx
If it goes wrong. If Copilot in Excel is unavailable, run the same prompts against the uploaded workbook in the Copilot app and note the difference in where the output lands.
The differentiator in this segment. The output is a changed file, not a message. That is the whole point of this segment.

Copilot in Teams: meeting recap

Copilot in Microsoft Teams, meeting recap and follow-up.
6minutes
Show the meeting turning into accountable actions without anyone taking minutes.
Prompts
Summarise this meeting for someone who did not attend. Separate what was decided from what was discussed but left open.
List every action, who owns it, and the date it is due. Flag any action that was assigned without a named owner or without a date.
Two decisions were sought in the pre-read. For each one, tell me whether it was actually decided, deferred, or never reached.
Draft the follow-up note to the committee. Neutral tone, no blame, and make the open items impossible to miss.
Talk track
  • Run the recap on a short live or recorded committee conversation.
  • The flagging prompt is the one that gets the nod. Every organisation has actions with no owner.
  • Show the draft follow-up but do not send it. Say that you are not sending it.
What to watch for
  • Decisions are separated from discussion.
  • Ownerless or dateless actions are called out rather than quietly recorded.
  • It notices when a sought decision was never actually reached.
Files
Portfolio_Performance_Committee_Agenda_and_Prereads.docx
If it goes wrong. Use a previously recorded meeting with transcription on. Confirm transcription and recap policy before the session.
The differentiator in this segment. This one does not exist outside the meeting platform. There is no prompt that replaces being in the room.

Agent Builder

Agent built in Microsoft 365 Copilot over governance documents.
7minutes
Show a business user, not a developer, building something governed and reusable.
Prompts
Build the agent live. Name it Bid Governance Adviser. Knowledge: the contract governance standard, the delegation of authority and the approval thresholds workbook. Instruction: answer only from these documents, always cite the clause, and say plainly when a proposed bid breaches a control.
Test it: a fixed price bid of 180 billion rupiah to a regional government client, with 5 percent contingency. Who approves it, and does it breach anything?
Test it again: we want to waive the independent estimate review to meet a tender deadline. Can we, and who can authorise that?
A subsidiary is at 47 percent fixed price share of its order book and wants to bid more. What does the standard require?
Talk track
  • Build it in front of them. The build is the demonstration, not the agent.
  • The second test should return a breach: 5 percent contingency against an 8 percent minimum.
  • The third test is the important one. The correct answer is that the review cannot be waived at all.
What to watch for
  • It cites the clause reference, not a paraphrase.
  • It refuses the waiver rather than finding a way to be helpful.
  • It applies the more restrictive of two overlapping thresholds.
Files
SOP-CG-004_Contract_Governance_Standard.docx Group_Delegation_of_Authority_Rev7.docx Bid_Approval_Thresholds.xlsx
If it goes wrong. Have the agent pre-built in a second window. Build the first half live, then switch.
The differentiator in this segment. The agent inherits tenant identity, permissions and audit. Ask what the equivalent looks like when the assistant sits outside the estate.

Cowork: multi-artefact pack with live data and web

Microsoft Copilot Cowork, with browser use and the analytics workspace.
10minutes
Show a whole workstream delivered, not a single answer.
Prompts
Take the working brief. Produce all five artefacts it asks for. Use the receivables pack and the contract data in the workspace for the evidence, and go and find current published construction cost indices to test the inflation explanation against an independent source.
The cluster says material cost inflation caused the margin decline. Test that claim against the data. If it does not hold, say so directly and show the comparison that disproves it.
Give me the one page version for the Ministry sponsor team. They are not construction specialists and they will read it once.
Talk track
  • Set expectations: this segment runs longer and produces several files. Let it work while you talk.
  • The moment to wait for is the disproof. It should compare fixed price against reimbursable in the same cost environment.
  • Close by opening two of the artefacts. Do not open all five.
What to watch for
  • It goes to the web for an independent cost index rather than trusting the internal claim.
  • It disproves the inflation explanation using the cost-plus comparison.
  • It produces genuinely different registers for the board and for the external sponsor.
Files
Cowork_Working_Brief_Remediation_Pack.docx Arkanusa_Receivables_and_Settlement_Pack.xlsx Fabric IQ dataset, 27 tables
If it goes wrong. Pre-run the pack the night before and keep the outputs ready. Run the disproof prompt live, since it is the shortest and most reliable.
The differentiator in this segment. Multi-artefact, multi-source, and every output lands in the estate where it is governed, retained and discoverable.

04The five questions that decide it

Do not present a feature comparison. Give them five questions to ask of any assistant, including the ones they already pay for, and let the answers do the work.

1.Can it see my work?

Why it decides the outcomeAn assistant that cannot see the work needs the work pasted into it. That is a tax on every single use, and it is paid by the person with the least time.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersGrounded across mail, chat, meetings, files and line of business data, with the user's own permissions applied to every retrieval.
What to invite them to askAsk what happens when the answer depends on a document the user has not found yet, and on a meeting they were not in.
Proved inSegment 2 · Microsoft 365 Copilot and Work IQ

2.Can it act where I work?

Why it decides the outcomeMost knowledge work is not producing text. It is changing a model, revising a deck, or turning a meeting into commitments.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersWorks inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams. The output is a changed file or a scheduled action, not a message to copy.
What to invite them to askAsk how many steps sit between the answer appearing and the file actually being updated.
Proved inSegment 5 · Edit in the app: Excel

3.Who can it act as, and who approved that?

Why it decides the outcomeThe moment an assistant takes action rather than giving advice, identity stops being a detail and becomes the control.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersAgents run with tenant identity, inherit permissions, and are created, approved and retired through admin controls the organisation already operates.
What to invite them to askAsk who provisioned the agent, whose data it can reach, and how it is decommissioned when its owner leaves.
Proved inSegment 7 · Agent Builder

4.Where does my data go, and can I prove it?

Why it decides the outcomeFor a state linked group this is not an IT question. It is a question the audit board will ask, and the answer has to be evidenced rather than asserted.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersPrompts and responses stay within the tenant boundary under enterprise data protection, and are subject to the same compliance, retention, eDiscovery and audit tooling as the rest of the estate.
What to invite them to askAsk to see the audit record of a prompt from six weeks ago, and whether it can be placed under legal hold.
Proved inSegment 7 · Agent Builder

5.What happens when I want five hundred of these?

Why it decides the outcomeOne team with a subscription is an experiment. Twenty three companies with different tools is a governance problem and a procurement problem at the same time.
How Microsoft 365 Copilot answersOne licence, one identity model, one compliance boundary, one admin surface, across every portfolio company.
What to invite them to askAsk what the estate looks like in eighteen months if each subsidiary picks its own assistant.

05The data story

Presenter reference. This is what the dataset proves and the order in which it proves it.

The headline. Infrastructure and Engineering EBITDA margin fell from 11.4 percent in FY2024 to 8.2 percent in FY2025 to 3.1 percent in FY2026 H1, while revenue grew 20 percent. The cluster reports the cause as construction material inflation. The data does not support that.
The decoy: Construction material cost inflation. Composite material and labour inflation over the period was 6.3 percent. It applies equally to cost-plus work, whose gross margin held at 9.5 percent while fixed-price work fell to minus 10.7 percent. A cost shock that hits one contract type and spares the other sitting next to it is not a cost shock.

The chain, in the order the data reveals it

1
Incentive

The FY2024 growth mandate rewarded order book intake, not delivered margin. Bid win rate rose from 22.4 percent to 41.3 percent.

2
Mix

The win rate was bought by moving to fixed-price lump-sum turnkey terms. LSTK share of won order value rose from 31 percent to 68 percent.

3
Execution

LSTK contracts run a mean cost variance of plus 19.0 percent against bid and a gross margin of minus 10.7 percent. Cost-plus contracts run plus 2.3 percent variance and plus 9.5 percent margin.

4
Concentration

Wisma Karya holds 72 percent of LSTK exposure by value. Regional government clients hold 64 percent of LSTK contract value.

5
Cash

Regional government days sales outstanding is 192 days against 60 days for private clients, and public sector settlement has slowed every year since FY2023. Receivables over 180 days grew from IDR 96 bn at December 2024 to IDR 623 bn at June 2026.

The verdict. This is a bidding discipline and incentive design problem that became a working capital problem. Repricing steel will not fix it. Changing what the bid committee is allowed to sign, and what the growth mandate rewards, will.
